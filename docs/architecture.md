# Architecture

llevant compiles an MCNP input into a read-only model and answers geometry questions
about it exactly and fast:

- where points are, and what lines cross;
- where rays first meet visible cells;
- slices and 3D views;
- volumes, and the gaps and overlaps of a model;
- fingerprints that tell two versions of a model apart.

It answers from the command line, from Python and in an interactive viewer. Results
of a transport run (per cell, per cell instance or on a mesh) can be drawn over the
geometry.

data flows, and how it is tested. Details:

## Why

Production MCNP models are large:

- 20 m long, with features down to millimetres;
- 10⁵–10⁶ surfaces and cells;
- several universe levels and lattices.

Questions about their geometry come up all the time, and MCNP answers them slowly or
not at all:

- Which cell is here?
- What does this line cross?
- What does this plane or this view look like?
- What is the volume of each cell and material?
- Where are the gaps and overlaps? MCNP finds them as lost particles, one at a time.
- What changed between two versions of a model?

In an envelope-based workflow (gitronics, or any similar one), a reactor model is
assembled from an envelope structure converted from CAD, with filler universes placed
inside the envelopes. Each new version can go wrong silently:

- an envelope that shrank clips its filler;
- one that grew leaves a gap;
- filler cells overlap;
- a transform is wrong.

Random points do not find these: a 1 cm³ change in 10¹⁰ cm³ needs ~10¹⁰ points and
still misses 1 mm gaps. Lines traced exactly do not miss them along their direction.
Tracing is many small, uneven tasks, which suits compiled code and threads sharing one
model; at 10⁶ surfaces, one copy of the model per process does not fit.

## Goals and non-goals

Goals:

- **Exact answers.** Exact line tracing (segments per cell at every level, with the
  surface crossed at each boundary). Points classified with their full path. Gaps and
  overlaps found while tracing.
- **Every MCNP geometry feature** the models use: universes, `FILL`/`*FILL`, `TRCL`,
  lattices, all macrobodies, nested to any depth.
- **Fingerprints** of each universe and cell, independent of numbering.
- **Scale.** The largest models load in seconds, and threads share one read-only
  model.
- **Views.** Slices with exact boundaries; 3D views with hidden cells and cutaways;
  realistic images; an interactive viewer that stays smooth on any model.
- **Volumes and checks.** Volumes per cell, material, universe or tally bin, with an
  honest uncertainty; every gap and overlap with its volume and location.
- **Frontends.** A CLI usable in CI, Python with a numpy batch API, and a desktop
  viewer whose views other programs can embed.

Non-goals:

- Writing MCNP: inputs are edited through migjorn's lossless tree (the viewer edits
  one card's text at a time); llevant writes no MCNP of its own.
- Transport or tallies: frontends draw results, but llevant computes none.
- Workflows across versions of a model (segment caches, policies about which
  envelopes may change): these belong to downstream tools.
- GPU computation outside the 3D view ([decision
  0001](decisions/0001-cpu-exact-gpu-approximate.md)).
- Matching the CAD.

Planned, not built yet:

- A persistent mode for scripts and agents (`llevant serve` over JSON lines, or an MCP
  server): a model loaded once and queried many times, answering in the CLI's JSON
  ([`guide/cli.md`](guide/cli.md#scripting-and-agents)). Python covers this today.

                                             ▼
                                          llevant  (engine: geometry only)
                                          ▲      ▲

                                          │      ▲  ◄── aci-tallies-geom, aci-tallies (crates.io)

                                          │      ▲      ▲

## Rules

- **The engine answers geometry questions only.** It knows nothing of images, GPUs,
  UIs, Python or the environment (no `std::env`). Options come in as `Options`.
- **The model is read-only after compiling.** What a frontend varies (material looks,
  visibility, cameras, view state, fields) is a value the frontend owns and passes in.
  An edit is a new model: `Model::recompile` against the old one, which reuses the
  universes whose content is unchanged ([decision
  0010](decisions/0010-staged-models-and-recompile.md)). The text goes through migjorn.
- **The fast path equals brute force.** Points get exactly the labels of
  `llevant::reference`. Lines agree with it as `reference::traces_agree` says. Boxes
  are conservative. [`contracts.md`](contracts.md) lists every guarantee and its test.
- **Results do not depend on the number of threads.** Results are written by index,
  never in the order threads finish.
- **MCNP is the reference for semantics.** Deliberate departures are listed in
  `internals/engine.md`, "Deliberate semantics".
- **Exact answers come from the CPU engine.** The GPU renderer is approximate by
  design and only draws ([decision 0001](decisions/0001-cpu-exact-gpu-approximate.md)).
- **Frontends use the public API.** `llevant::internals` (hidden, unstable) serves
  `llevant-gpu`, `llevant-bench` and the oracle. What a frontend needs from the engine
  becomes an engine feature, designed and tested there, never worked around in the
  frontend.
- **Shared frontend code goes down a layer, not sideways.** What the CLI, Python and
  the viewer all need lives in `llevant-scene`, `llevant-render` or `llevant-gpu`.
- **The viewer's UI thread does no model-sized work.** It reads state, swaps `Arc`s
  and buffers, and lays out what is on screen. Anything that scales with cells,
  instances, voxels or pixels runs on the deriver, a task, a worker or a pool of its
  own, and the views keep showing what they had until the result lands ([decision
  0008](decisions/0008-ui-thread-rule.md)). `llevant-viewer --storm` checks it.

## Data flow

```
 MCNP input ──migjorn──► compile ──► Model ──save/read──► disk cache (llevant-scene::store)
                                       │
            ┌──────────────┬───────────┼──────────────┬────────────────┐
            ▼              ▼           ▼              ▼                ▼
        classify         trace     first_hits     fingerprints      volumes
        (labels)      (segments)    (hits)        (digests)     (estimates, problems)
            │              │           │
            │      slices, labels  render ◄── Visibility (llevant-scene::hiding)
            ▼              ▼           ▼
      CLI tables     llevant-render: Paint (colours, Looks, field) ──► PNG, Python Image
                                       │
                         Model::export ──► llevant-gpu ──► viewer G-buffer ──► passes ──► window
```

1. **Load.** `Store::load` hashes the input, the `Options` and the engine build. It
   reads the compiled model from the cache, or else recompiles it against the last
   version of the same path (or compiles it), and stores it ([decision
   0004](decisions/0004-model-cache.md), [decision
   0010](decisions/0010-staged-models-and-recompile.md)). The viewer loads staged: it
   shows the model once its BVHs are built and swaps in the complete one when its
   octrees are.
2. **Compile.** migjorn parses; llevant resolves `LIKE BUT`, transforms, macrobody
   facets, fills and lattices into flat arrays. Then it builds boxes, a BVH per
   universe, octrees with residual programs where boxes overlap a lot ([decision
   0002](decisions/0002-residual-programs.md)), and the problem box ([decision
   0003](decisions/0003-problem-box.md)).
3. **Query.** Every query takes a batch and returns flat arrays: label ids interned
   per batch, ready for numpy.
4. **Draw.** `llevant-render` colours labels and hits with a `Paint`. The GPU renderer
   reads `Model::export` and traces camera rays in short slices ([decision
   0005](decisions/0005-sliced-gpu-dispatches.md)). The viewer reprojects the hits it
   has every frame ([decision 0006](decisions/0006-reprojection-gpu-only.md)) and runs
   a chain of WGSL passes over them.
5. **Edit.** The viewer writes an edited card into migjorn's tree and recompiles against
   the model shown. While a surface is dragged, a `Draft` previews it: the cells,
   rois and BVHs it touches are updated in a few ms, and the GPU copy is patched in
   place. The edit itself compiles on release ([decision
   0011](decisions/0011-live-drag-drafts.md)).

## Frontends

- **CLI** (`llevant`): `info`, `bbox`, `locate`, `trace`, `slice`, `render`, `materials`,
  `fingerprint [--diff]`, `volume`, `check`, `view`. It prints tables as text, CSV or
  JSON, and writes PNG images; `check` exits with 1 on gaps or overlaps.
  [`guide/cli.md`](guide/cli.md).
- **Python** (`import llevant`): a `Model` kept in memory. Batch queries take
  anything `numpy.asarray` accepts and return read-only numpy columns that Rust built,
  without holding the GIL. `Image`s show inline in Jupyter. `python -m llevant view`
  opens the viewer from the wheel. [`guide/python.md`](guide/python.md).
- **Viewer** (`llevant-viewer`): the 3D view and an exact slice side by side, with
  tools, rules, undo, fields, FMESH layout and analyses as background tasks.
  Its use in [`reference/viewer.md`](reference/viewer.md); embedding and the application's
  structure in [its README](https://github.com/Fusion4Energy/llevant/blob/main/crates/llevant-viewer/README.md); `internals/rendering.md`,
  "The viewer".

## Testing

- **Against MCNP** (`tools/mcnp-oracle`): random points are classified by llevant and
  by MCNP, and must agree. Confirmed points become golden tables that `cargo test`
  replays. No disagreement so far ([`mcnp-behaviour.md`](mcnp-behaviour.md)).
- **Fast path against brute force**: property tests on random models, in every
  `GridMode`, with and without residual programs; the reference model in `--ignored`
  tests; `llevant-bench verify` and `compare-trace`.
- **Between queries**: points along traced segments classify to their labels, and
  first hits are the first visible segments.
- **Volumes**: analytic volumes, planted gaps and overlaps, and an honest sigma.
- **Determinism**: identical results with 1 and N threads.
- **Images**: GPU against CPU ray by ray; the viewer's images against `llevant render`;
  reprojection against fresh traces.
- **The viewer application**, driven headlessly with egui_kittest, and the edit storm.
- **CI** (`.github/workflows`):
  - fmt, clippy, and tests on Linux and Windows;
  - the GPU and viewer tests on lavapipe, and the renderer's shader compiled by DXC on D3D12 (WARP, nothing rendered) on Windows;
  - the wheel with pytest;
  - MSRV and `cargo deny`.
- **Nightly**: random proptest seeds, and the work-counter regression on the
  synthetic models (`tools/bench/work-baseline.json`).

Test models are built in code, or kept small in `tools/mcnp-oracle/models`. The
reference model (425 k cells, not in git) runs in `--ignored` tests and benchmarks.
Synthetic models of other shapes (tokamak sectors, a lattice core, a sparse world,
bricks, torus shells) keep tuning from fitting one model family.

## Roadmap

- **Engine.**
  - Volumes per voxel of a mesh.
  - Faster fillers (level 1 now dominates the reference model).
  - Cheaper volume convergence: `--rel-sigma` down to the default `--h-min` (h / 64)
    on the reference model would take about two hours, nearly all of it tracing
    pieces of groups that never converge.
  - CPU first hits in sparse worlds (`sparse.i`) are slower than full traces of the
    same rays, from the doubling windows across a 5 km world.
  - Dedicated tests for tangent lines and near-coincident surfaces.
- **Viewer.**
  - Numeric fields for surface and `TR` coefficients in the card editor.
  - Its visual design.
  - Hiding and selecting by instance and by value.
  - Pins hidden behind geometry drawn faded.
- **Downstream.**
  - Comparing two versions of a model: segments traced on shared lines, only where
    fingerprints differ.
  - A stable identity for envelopes across conversions, from `$` tags.
- **Later.** A browser plotter (WebGPU, or the engine in WebAssembly).

Open questions:

- Fingerprint stability across two real conversions of the same envelope.
- The tolerance for "surface noise" (surfaces moved by ~1e-5 cm by a new conversion).
- Whether the reference model's overlaps are real defects. `llevant check --h 5` finds
  1 086 overlap labels (0.6 m³); an MCNP check of a few points would settle it.
